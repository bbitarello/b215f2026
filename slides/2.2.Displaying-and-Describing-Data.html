Reality check: A note about modes

  • for many real-world datasets, it is common to have no observations with the same value in a dataset, making this definition impractical in data analysis.

Modes as peaks in distributions

Width (aka variability)

Variability of a population should not be ignored as simply noise about the mean, but is biologically important in its own right.

Variation has a true value from a population that we estimate from a sample.

Range

The difference between the maximum and minimum observed values in a sample

  • The range of a sample corresponds to the size of the narrowest interval containing all the data.

  • Operationally, it’s calculated as the difference between the maximum and minimum.

Interquartile Range (IQR)

The difference between the 75th and 25th percentiles of the data. A.k.a., the “middle 50%” of the data. . Can sometimes be expressed as an interval, though less common.

Imagine sorting your data.

  • The individual in the middle is the median.
  • The first and last individuals mark the range
  • The other two quantiles are the individuals ¼ and ¾ the way into your sorted list of data
  • The difference between these is the interquartile range
  • less biased than range

Recommended: https://psu-eberly.shinyapps.io/Effect_of_Outliers/

But there are metrics that look at all the values

  • Variance, Standard Deviation, & Coefficient of Variation all communicate how far individual observations are expected to deviate from the mean.

  • Because (by definition) the differences between all observations and the mean sum to zero (), these summaries work from the squared difference between observations and the mean ().

In summary

Range: The difference between the largest and smallest values. Underestimates population range (biased estimator).

Interquartile Range: The difference between the 25th and 75th percentile.

Variance & Standard Deviation: The difference between observations & the mean.

Coefficient of Variation: A measure of the standard deviation that’s independent of the mean.

Coefficient of Variation (CV)

  • The variance and standard deviation increase with the mean.

  • The coefficient of variation allows for fair comparisons of variability between measures on different scales.

Rounding

  • Computers can return values with precision that exceeds biological interest.
  • When sharing results, round numbers to a reasonable number of significant figures.
  • Presenting one or two more digits than initially measured is a good rule of thumb.
  • DO NOT round while calculating or sharing intermediate results!
  • Round only the final results of the specific summaries of interest.
