In the face of chance (randomness of my sample),

can I trust my estimate?

And how much can I trust it?

Two key measures of precision of an estimate

  • Standard Error

  • Confidence Intervals

The Standard Error: Definition

Definition: The standard error predicts the sampling error of the estimate.

  • The standard error predicts the sampling error of the estimate.

  • We can calculate SE for any summary statistic

  • Today we focus on the most common – the standard error of the mean

  • Later we will also learn SE estimators for proportions, odds ratios, and risk ratios.

The Standard Error of the Mean (SEM)

The SEM quantifies how precisely you know the population mean

  • Exact value: Because we rarely know the population standard deviation,\(\sigma\), we cannot find the parameter \(\sigma_{\bar{X}}=\frac{\sigma_{x}}{\sqrt{n}}\)

  • But, we can use the sample standard deviation \(s\), to estimate \(SEM_{\bar{X}}=\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}\)

  • Assumption: sample is random.

Note we use SEM instead of SE to avoid ambiguities. Standard errors can be calculated for other statistics besides the mean.

Plotting the Sampling Distribution

Compare: population vs. sampling distribution

  • Mean parameter estimates of a sampling distribution should equal population parameters.

  • The width (standard deviation) of a population (\(\sigma=\) 2833) exceeds that of its sampling distribution (\(s_{\bar{x}}=\) 280).

Compare: SD of sampling distribution to \(\sigma/\sqrt{n}\) to \(s/\sqrt{n}\)

  • The standard deviation of this distribution (sample size 100) is 280 nucleotides

  • So 280 is our estimate for the standard error of the mean based on our sampling distribution

  • Meanwhile, \(\sigma_{\bar{X}}=\sigma/\sqrt{n}=283\)

  • And the estimate from one sample of 100 genes (slide 12): \(SEM_{\bar{X}}=2712/\sqrt(100)=271\)

\(SEM_{\bar{X}}\) is an estimate of \(\sigma_{\bar{x}}\) and is itself estimated with error.

The Standard Error & The Sampling Distribution

  • You might be thinking, but how would I get such a sampling distribution?

  • It turns out there is a clever way of resampling from your own sample to estimate an (unknown) sampling distribution!

Bootstrapping!

Not all is lost: bootstrapping

Even if you can’t repeatedly go to the population and sample over and over again …

By pretending that the sample itself represents the population, which allows one to approximate the effect of sampling variability by resampling (with replacementt) from the original sample. This is “bootstrap resampling” or, simply, “bootstrapping”.

More on this later in lab.

When is bootstrap useful?

Always!

But… it is computationally intensive so:

  1. When no formula (equation) is available for the SE of a statistic of interest

  2. When the sampling distribution of an estimate of interest is unknown.

More on this later in lab.

Sample size, uncertainty, and the standard error

SE Decreases with Sample Size

The standard error goes down as a sample size goes up.

Small Samples, Large Uncertainty

Treat simple summary statistics with skepticism.

Be sure to consider uncertainty before being misled by exceptional values.

tibble(sample_size = seq(5,

Extreme estimate values are often associated with small sample size.

How we report SEM

  • \(\bar{x}\pm SEM\)

  • E.g., if your sample has a mean of \(\bar{x}=2300\) and \(s_{x}=1300\), so \(SEM_{\bar{X}}=\frac{1300}{\sqrt{10}}\approx411.1\)

So we would report \(2,300 \pm411.1\text{ (SEM)}\). Later we will also see how the \(SEM\) is used in plots (error bars).

Standard error - Summary

  • A standard error is just the standard deviation of a statistic

  • We use “standard error” to talk about the distribution of a statistic, whereas “standard deviation” is usually used to talk about the distribution of data itself (either the whole population or a sample)

  • Small samples underestimate the SD of the population

  • For huge sample sizes, the SEM is always tiny, even if the data have considerable spread (SD).

  • Estimates with smaller standard errors are more precise

  • The smaller the sampling error, the less uncertainty (due to random sampling) there is about the target parameter in the population (“out there”).

  • You can calculate the standard error for any estimate, not just the mean.

  • The SE of the sample mean if sometimes referred to as SE, but this is ambiguous - use SEM instead of SE

  • Both SD and SEM are expressed in the same unit as the data
